Hear the Primes

Each nontrivial zero of the Riemann zeta function adds one wave, cos(γ ln x). Add enough of them and the waves pile up exactly on the primes. Move the slider, then press Play bar to hear the number line from 1.5 to 31.5.

What you are hearing

  • DroneOne sine per zero, pitched in proportion to γ (the first zero, γ₁ = 14.1347, is E2). The zeros are not a harmonic series, so the chord is metallic.
  • PrimesPlucked strings at the peaks that land on a prime. Pitch is that harmonic of G2 folded into one octave: 3 is a fifth, 5 a major third, 7 a harmonic seventh.
  • PowersBells at 4, 8, 9, 16, 25, 27: the smaller spikes at prime powers. Their weight is 1/k of a prime's, so they ring softer.
  • GhostsPeaks that sit between integers because too few zeros are active. They play out of tune and disappear as you add zeros.
ψ′(x) = 1 − 2x−1/2 Σγ cos(γ ln x) − 1/(x(x²−1))
