The music of the primes

Zeta Prime Music

Every zero of the Riemann zeta function is a wave. Add them one by one and the primes come into tune. A composition, a video and a playable instrument, generated note for note from the mathematics.

Critical Strip

A piece in five movements. One bar of the number line, from 1.5 to 31.5, is replayed while zeros are added along the Fibonacci numbers. Out-of-tune notes slide onto the primes; prime powers ring as softer bells; the drone is the zeros themselves.

How it works

From zeros to notes in four steps

Riemann's explicit formula writes the primes as a sum of waves, one per zero. The piece makes each part of that formula audible.

One zero, one wave

The zero ρ = ½ + 14.1347i contributes cos(14.13 ln x): a wave that stretches as x grows. It becomes the lowest drone tone.

Waves add up

With a handful of zeros the sum wobbles, with broad bumps between the integers. These play as out-of-tune ghost notes.

Spikes at the primes

With 200 zeros the waves cancel everywhere except at 2, 3, 5, 7, 11 … and, smaller, at 4, 8, 9, 16, 25, 27.

Spikes become music

Each prime is plucked at the n-th harmonic of G: 3 is a fifth, 5 a major third, 7 a harmonic seventh. Prime powers ring as bells.

ψ′(x) = 1 − 2x−1/2 Σγ cos(γ ln x) − 1/(x(x²−1))

A tuning process

Movement II is a process piece: the same bar twelve times, each with more zeros. You hear the mathematics converge as intonation.

Arithmetic dynamics

Loudness is the spike height: full at primes, −6 dB at squares, −9.5 dB at cubes. The accent pattern is the arithmetic of the numbers.

Two pitch worlds

An inharmonic drone rooted on E (the zeros) under a just-intoned melody in G (the primes): E minor and G major at once.

Open source

Render it yourself

A tested Python engine (numpy, scipy, mpmath) with 500 bundled zeros. Scores are TOML files: change the bar length, the key, the number of zeros, and render your own version. MIDI export keeps every microtonal bend.
