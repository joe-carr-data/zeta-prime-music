Guide for musicians

How Critical Strip is made

Tuning, harmony, rhythm, dynamics and form, with just enough mathematics to see where every note comes from. No note is placed by hand: the composer's decisions are the mappings, and the mathematics fills in the rest.

1 · The idea

Riemann's explicit formula writes the primes as a sum of waves. Each nontrivial zero of the zeta function, ρ = ½ + iγ, contributes one wave, cos(γ ln x), along the number line. One zero gives a smooth wobble. Add a few hundred and the waves cancel almost everywhere, except at 2, 3, 4, 5, 7, 8, 9, 11, 13 …, where they pile up into sharp spikes: the primes (tall) and the prime powers (shorter).

The central gesture: the same bar is replayed while zeros are added. You hear wrong notes slide into tune until the bar plays the primes exactly.

2 · The drone: zeros as a spectrum

Each zero becomes a sine partial at f = 82.41 Hz × γ / γ₁, so the first zero is E2 (the lowest guitar string) and every other zero keeps its exact ratio to it. The ratios are not a harmonic series, so the drone is inharmonic, like a bell or a gong. That is why it sounds metallic.

It gets denser as it rises. Near the bottom the zeros are about 7.8 units of γ apart, near γ = 100 about 2.3, near γ = 400 about 1.5 (the average spacing is 2π / ln(γ/2π)). The low partials stand apart as a chord; the high ones crowd into a shimmering cluster.

Zeros avoid each other. Their spacings follow the same statistics as the energy levels of heavy atomic nuclei (the GUE law of random matrices): two zeros almost never sit very close together, so the cluster has fewer very slow beats than a random cluster of the same density.

An observation rather than a design choice: the first six zeros land near E, B, D, F, G, A, an E minor seventh with an added 11th and a flat 9th, the colour of the Phrygian mode. Since each zero's loudness is 1/|ρ|, this low chord dominates what you hear. Each partial also has a slow tremolo that follows its own wave cos(γ ln x) at the current point on the number line; higher zeros pulse faster.

3 · The melody: primes as harmonics

Every spike sits on a whole number n. The piece hears n as the n-th harmonic of G2 (98 Hz), folded down by octaves into one octave and then raised one octave for every factor of 22.5 ≈ 5.7 in n (at most three), so small numbers sound low and larger ones higher. The primes become the prime-limit intervals of just intonation.

  • Prime powers are literally overtones of their prime: 4, 8, 16 are the tonic in higher octaves, 9 the ninth, 27 the Pythagorean sixth.

4 · How the two pitch worlds fit

The drone's root E2 and the melody's tonic G2 are 300 cents apart, exactly a minor third. Put the melody's G–B–D over the drone's E and you get E minor 7 (E–G–B–D), the same chord the first zeros sketch on their own.

So the piece hovers between E minor (the drone) and G major (the plucked primes), the relative-key pair, while the higher primes and the inharmonic partials keep both from settling into a common-practice key.

5 · Tuning as a process

With few zeros the spikes are broad and their peaks sit between the integers. The piece plays each peak at its exact position, so it comes out as a microtonal ghost. In the first bar, with a single zero:

Each time zeros are added, the peaks narrow (their width shrinks roughly like x / N) and slide onto the integers. Ghosts that sit on no prime fade out; new peaks appear. By 34 zeros every note of the bar is a prime or prime power within a few cents. Movement II is a slow tuning-in: a process piece in the sense of Steve Reich's phase music, where the process is mathematical convergence. Try it in the instrument.

6 · Rhythm: a sieve on a 30-step grid

The bar sweeps x from 1.5 to 31.5 in 8 seconds, so every whole number has its own moment: 3.75 numbers per second, a 30-step sequencer. The active steps are the primes and prime powers.

Movement III leaves the bar and sweeps x exponentially from 31.5 to 260, so each second covers the same ratio of the number line. Measured that way primes get denser further out, so the notes speed up: an accelerando built into the number line.

7 · Dynamics come from arithmetic

The height of a spike, after normalisation, is Λ(n)/ln n: 1 at a prime, ½ at a square, ⅓ at a cube. That is used directly as loudness: primes at full level, squares (4, 9, 25) at −6 dB, cubes (8, 27) at −9.5 dB, 16 at −12 dB (often too quiet to be detected at all, because its spike really is the smallest). Prime powers also play on bells mixed 4 dB under the plucks, so relative to a prime they sound at −10, −13.5 and −16 dB. The accent pattern of every bar is the arithmetic of its numbers.

With a fixed number of zeros, primes far along the number line are resolved less sharply and come out softer, about −2.6 dB by x ≈ 220 with 500 zeros, so movement III fades slightly towards the edge of what the zeros can resolve.

The alternative versions change only these parameters: Prime Pulse uses a 5-second bar; Deep Strip a 12-second bar with the drone an octave lower; Critical Strip (revised) turns IV into a call and response and runs V backwards so the piece ends on 3 then 2.

11 · Make your own

With your own instruments. Every render writes a MIDI file. Each note carries its exact microtonal pitch as a pitch bend (±2 semitones) on its own channel while it sounds, so it works with any MPE synth. Time is in seconds (60 BPM).

Writing a score. A score is a TOML file; musically its parameters mean:

ParameterMusical meaning
loopbar length (tempo): 8 s gives 3.75 numbers per second
x_start, x_endwhich stretch of the number line the bar plays
zeros_stepshow in tune each bar is; the sequence is the shape of the process
sweep = "exp"accelerando through the number line
pluck_base_hzkey of the melody, e.g. 98 Hz = G
drone_base_hzroot of the drone, e.g. 82.41 Hz = E
drone_gain, pluck_gain, breath_gainper-section orchestration (0 mutes a voice)

Try moving the melody's key against the drone: a minor third apart (the default) gives the E minor / G major ambiguity; a unison makes the drone's root the tonic of the primes; a tritone makes the two worlds clash.
